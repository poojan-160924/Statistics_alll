SESSION 1 – Introduction to Statistics in Data Science

TASK 1: List 5 daily actions you perform on apps like Instagram, Zomato,
or Flipkart, and identify whether the data generated is numerical or
categorical.

Answer: 1. Instagram – Liking a post: The number of likes is numerical
data. 2. Instagram – Following someone: The username or profile category
is categorical data. 3. Zomato – Rating a restaurant: The rating (1 to 5
stars) is numerical data. 4. Flipkart – Adding products to a cart:
Product names and categories are categorical data. 5. Zomato – Ordering
food: The order amount is numerical data.

TASK 2: Given this sample data from a music app: [‘Pop’, ‘Rock’, ‘Jazz’,
‘Hip-Hop’, ‘Pop’, ‘Rock’, ‘Jazz’, ‘Pop’], classify the type of data and
explain your reasoning in 2–3 lines.

Answer: The given data is categorical data (Nominal Data) because it
represents different music genres such as Pop, Rock, Jazz, and Hip-Hop.
These categories do not have any specific numerical value or order. We
can use the mode to identify the most popular music genre.

TASK 3: Pick any one feature from an app you use (such as the number of
likes on an Instagram post, or the rating of a restaurant on Zomato) and
describe how statistics could help improve that feature for users.

Answer: I choose the number of likes on Instagram posts.

Statistics can help Instagram understand which types of posts receive
more likes. By analyzing the average likes, user engagement, and posting
time, Instagram can identify users’ interests. This information can help
Instagram recommend more interesting content and improve the user
experience.

TASK 4: Suppose you are building a new playlist recommendation feature
for a Spotify-like app. List 3 basic statistical terms (such as mean,
median, mode, variance, etc.) that would be useful in analyzing user
listening data, and explain each with a one-line example.

Answer: 1. Mean (Average): It helps calculate the average listening time
of users. For example, if a user listens for 2, 3, and 4 hours on three
days, the average listening time is 3 hours. 2. Median: It helps find
the middle value of listening time. For example, if listening times are
10, 20, and 30 minutes, the median is 20 minutes. 3. Mode: It helps
identify the most frequently played song or music genre. For example, if
a user listens to Pop, Rock, Pop, and Jazz, the mode is Pop.

Conclusion: These statistical terms help understand user listening
habits and recommend songs and playlists according to their interests.
